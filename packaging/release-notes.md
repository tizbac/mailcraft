**MailCraft 0.14.0 in short**: new features and fixes for the public beta (testers welcome; sign up in [#11](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/11))
- Send later, Undo Send, and follow-up reminders that come back when nobody replies ([#950](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/950), [#1363](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1363), [#948](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/948)).
- Warnings before a message goes to a look-alike address or shows many outside addresses to each other, and sender warnings for mail posing as a well-known brand ([#509](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/509), [#1208](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1208)).
- Unsubscribe from a mailing list from the bar above its message, and react to a message with one emoji ([#1207](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1207), [#1544](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1544)).
- On a Microsoft account, categories are kept in step with Outlook, and sign-in asks for what MailCraft uses on one page ([#661](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/661), [#1594](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1594)).
- An IMAP account can send from several addresses; subscribe to calendars on the web; an Exchange server account searches the organisation's address list ([#797](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/797), [#1262](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1262)).
- Designed mail shows the pictures behind its text, Hebrew and Arabic mail stands at the right, and the files inside `winmail.dat` are listed ([#601](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/601), [#603](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/603), [#1232](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1232)).
- Polish, Czech, Georgian and Hungarian as preview languages ([#1357](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1357), [#1010](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1010), [#1442](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/pull/1442)).
- Sturdier: one message that can't be downloaded no longer stops Send/Receive, and a mailbox file that can't be read at start is never replaced by an empty one ([#1581](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1581), [#1425](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1425)).
- And more fixes, listed below.

**What's new**
- New mail on an IMAP account shows within moments where the server supports IDLE, and Send/Receive passes over folders that haven't changed ([#276](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/276)).
- Add Account finds the servers of a domain it doesn't know from the domain's DNS, its autoconfig document or Exchange's Autodiscover ([#1063](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1063), [#1112](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1112), [#1103](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1103)).
- Gmail labels show on messages, and `label:` finds them ([#1153](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1153)).
- Close to system tray, names of your own for accounts and shared mailboxes, and the Mail, Calendar, People and Tasks switch at the foot of the folder pane ([#544](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/544), [#1017](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1017), [#1385](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1385), [#1174](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1174)).
- A neutral grey dark theme, with the blue grey one still a choice ([#1384](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1384)); Romanian and Simplified Chinese as preview languages ([#1135](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1135), [#1031](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1031)).
- The browser build starts again, and a JMAP account sends mail and takes your changes up to its server ([#1337](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1337), [#1074](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1074)).
- A message prints as pages, not as plain text: its header, formatting, tables and pictures, on the printer and the sheet you choose (File › Print).
- Sturdier: a virus scanner taking part of a message no longer stops every save, and a large mailbox takes far less memory ([#1311](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1311), [#1340](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1340)).
- An Exchange server account sends the changes made here a hundred to a request instead of one request each, so marking tens of thousands of messages read goes up in hundreds of requests. Send/Receive Progress shows how many of them the server has taken on — a bar that moves, "Folders: 3,000 of 10,000" — instead of a spinner that says only that the run is at the folders. When a folder's answer holds a change that can't be taken in, the line now names the item by its subject when the server sent one.
- An attachment's chip can be dragged out of the reading pane or a message window onto the desktop (#626): Explorer on Windows, or a file manager on X11 or Wayland, receives the file under the name Save As gives it, marked as from the Internet.
- **Printing a message, as pages.** File › Print (Ctrl+P) prints the selected message on the printer you choose: its subject, sender, recipients, date and file names, then its text with headings, bold and italic, colours, lists, quotes, rules, tables and pictures, on A4 or Letter, every page numbered at its foot. The Print dialog asks for the printer, the copies, collating, two sided and black and white. MailCraft sets the pages itself; the system's own spooler takes them (CUPS on Linux, macOS and the BSDs, the Windows print spooler on Windows), so nothing else is opened and nothing in a message runs. A picture written into the message or attached to it is printed; one on the web is printed only if you had let pictures download and the window already has it, and one with no pictures at all prints the words that say what it is. Hidden sender text is never printed. The calendar, contacts and tasks are not printed yet, and the browser build has no printer to spool to.
- A message can be dragged out of the list to a folder of the desktop — Explorer on Windows, a file manager on X11 or Wayland — where it is written as the format File › Options chooses (`.eml` or `.msg`), named from its subject.
- **Save Message As writes Outlook `.msg` files.** File › Save As can save a message as a `.msg` file Outlook reads: its subject, text and HTML body, sender, To/Cc/Bcc, date, message id and header block, with one entry for each recipient and each attachment (by value, with its name, media type and content id). File › Options › Save Message As chooses whether `.eml` or `.msg` is the default; the command takes `"format": "msg"` or `"format": "eml"` to override it for one save. Reading a `.msg` file is not built yet.
- An Exchange server account's calendar is read a month back and a year ahead at Send/Receive, and the days you go to in the calendar beyond that are read by themselves, so they no longer stay empty until the next Send/Receive ([#1274](https://github.com/UCS-Care-Group/mailcraft/issues/1274)).
- OpenPGP Digital signatures are now checked in the reading pane; Public keys can be imported, listed and removed ([#30](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/30)).
- Outgoing mail can be Digitally signed: Options › Sign in a new message, or Always for an account under its Digital Signatures, with an S/MIME certificate from the system's certificate store, keychain or a smartcard, or an OpenPGP key in GnuPG (a smartcard too). A message that can't be signed is held in the Outbox and never sent unsigned ([#30](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/30)).
- The limits on importing mail files are yours to set in File › Options: the largest file (512 MB by default), the most messages from one file (200,000), and the largest and most messages of a folder of mailboxes (4 GB and 500,000), with a note on what they cost in memory.
- A shared mailbox can be opened under an Exchange server account as it can under a Microsoft account: Add Shared Mailbox reads its folders and mail at its own address with the owner's sign-in, a message there can be read in full, and mail written in it is sent from its address when the server allows Send As or Send on Behalf, with the copy filed in its own Sent Items ([#70](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/70)).
- Exchange server accounts keep their calendars, address books and to-do lists in step, both ways, after the mail of a Send/Receive: appointments and meetings (including the people invited, the place, the notes and the reminder), contacts (with their addresses, phone numbers, notes and birthday) and to-dos (with their dates, importance and completion). Another person's calendar or address book can be opened by address when they have shared it, and is read with the owner's sign-in; it is listed under Shared Calendars or with the account's books. Tried against a Grommunio server; Exchange Server itself has been tried for mail and reading only.
- Linchange accounts: Add Account has a Linchange tile, and such an account takes the mail exchanger of its domain as its server. It signs in through Linchange's own page in the browser and keeps an application password named for this computer, so the account password and its second factor never go to the mail client.
- Choose an action for swiping left or right over a message: delete, archive, mark as read, flag, or none. The row follows the gesture and reveals its action; a short swipe returns it. Both directions start off. On Windows, horizontal mouse-wheel scrolling can also trigger the chosen action.
- Save Message As (File, or a message's right-click menu) saves the message as it was received, byte for byte, so that a Digital signature still checks in the file: for mail of a Microsoft, Exchange server or JMAP account it downloads the server's own copy first, and a signed or encrypted message is saved from the bytes it came as. A message that can't be had that way is saved from what this computer keeps, and the status bar says so; one of which only the headers are here is no longer saved as if it were whole ([#1561](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1561)).
- Signing in with Microsoft asks for what MailCraft uses on one page, shared mailboxes and their calendars included (not for a personal account, which has none, and Outlook's Favorites are still asked for by themselves), instead of asking again the first time you open a shared mailbox, send from one or tick one of its calendars. An account that signed in before is asked once, in one question, for whatever it doesn't have yet ([#1594](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1594)).
- Android: the Calendar tab has a Week view, and a swipe left or right turns to the next or previous day, week or month.
- Android: the calendar, your contacts and Microsoft To Do are kept up to date by the phone on its own, in the background too, along with your mail; To Do only after you allowed it, and reminders follow what changed.
- React with one emoji: a button beside Forward opens 👍 ❤️ 🎉 😂 😮 😢, and one click sends a short reaction to the sender (the address is shown beside the emoji; not for mail with a sender warning), as a normal mail filed in Sent Items (for IMAP, JMAP and local accounts; a local account delivers it only to a local account the address names; [#1544](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1544)).

- Android: the reading pane shows the pictures that are part of a message, and downloads pictures from the web only when you say so (this message, a sender or a domain), holds them back while Data Saver is on until you tap, and can show a sender's picture beside each row once you turn that on in Settings.

- **Agents can see whether a message passed its checks.** Reading a message over MCP (or `mail.get`) now says what your mail server found for SPF, DKIM and DMARC, taken only from the server's own result and left out when it gave none; the signing domain is shown only when it is up to 100 bytes long. The command search for agents also looks in the parameter text, so searching for `header` finds View Source ([#1472](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1472)).
- Selecting an attachment opens a ribbon for opening, saving or removing the file. Available previews ask for confirmation below the message header, while other attachments leave the message visible. Show Message is available when there is a preview to return from.
- **Follow-up reminders.** Follow Up › Add Reminder… flags a message with a date and time; the reminders window raises it then (Dismiss, Snooze, a double click opens the message), once, also if MailCraft was closed at the time. For mail you sent, and in the message you are writing (Follow Up), "Remind me if nobody replies" after 1, 2, 3, 5 or 7 days or on a date: if nobody the message went to has written back in its conversation by then, the reminder is raised and the message flagged; a reply that arrives first cancels it. Clearing the flag, marking the message complete or deleting it ends the reminder. The reminder time is kept on this computer only; the flag syncs with the account as before ([#948](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/948)).
- Send later ([#950](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/950), [#1363](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1363)): Delay Delivery in the Message tab, and Send Later… beside the Send button, hold a message in the Outbox until a date and time you choose. - Calendars on the web can be subscribed to: Calendar › Open Calendar › From Internet… takes an https:// or webcal:// address and keeps a read-only copy up to date every hour (you can choose 15 minutes to a day in File › Info), under Other Calendars. - **Files inside `winmail.dat`.** Mail sent from Outlook in its own rich-text format carries its files wrapped in one `winmail.dat` attachment; MailCraft now lists the files inside it (with their names and types) beside it, and uses its text as the message's when the message has none of its own. Nothing in it runs or is fetched, and an attached message or object inside it stays in the `winmail.dat`. Mail already in a mailbox is unchanged until it is downloaded again ([#1232](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1232)).
- Unsubscribe from a mailing list from the bar above one of its messages, or its right-click menu: in one click where the list allows it (RFC 8058), by a message from your account, or on the list's own page in your browser. MailCraft asks first and says what is sent and to whom, and offers it only for mail your mail server confirms came from the list, outside Junk E-mail and with no warning about its sender ([#1207](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1207)).

- **Warnings before sending.** MailCraft asks before a message goes to an address that looks like someone you write to but isn't (a domain, or the part before the @, one character away from a contact's or from someone you've sent mail to), and before 5 or more people outside your organisation, at 2 or more domains, would see each other's addresses in To and Cc (with personal accounts only, there's no organisation, and this isn't asked). You can send anyway, cancel, or, for the outsiders, move them to Bcc in one click. A Reply All to people who were already on the message together isn't asked about. When an agent sends, you're asked in the window and the agent can't answer for you; a script without the window is refused unless it says to send anyway ([#509](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/509)).
- **Pictures behind the text of designed mail.** A newsletter or a piece of stationery that puts a picture behind a table, a cell or the whole message (a banner photograph with a headline on it, a paper texture, a gradient) now shows it, at the size and place it asks for, once or repeated. A picture that is attached to the message or written into it is shown straight away; one on the web is never downloaded for a background, whatever you allow (only the message's pictures proper are), so it cannot tell its sender the message was read. Where the picture is shown as it is over a colour the sender gave the box, the text colours the sender wrote there are kept (they are meant for the picture, which is not looked at, not for the colour under it, so white text over a dark banner stays white); where the sender gave no colour, the picture is softened so its text stays readable, and in the dark theme the picture is dimmed and its text is made readable against the colour turned dark, as before. Dark or default-coloured text over a dark picture is not changed: the sender's text colour is trusted, and the program does not look at the picture's colours. With high contrast on, the picture is left out. Nothing hidden in the message, and no picture behind a box of a pixel or two, is shown ([#601](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/601)).
- **Categories kept in step with Outlook.** On a Microsoft account (not one still on IMAP), a category you make, delete, rename or give another colour in MailCraft now changes in Outlook too, a few seconds later (one you delete, once your mail, events and to-dos there no longer carry it), and what you change in Outlook shows in MailCraft within a quarter of an hour; before, a category you deleted or renamed here came back. A rename gives your mail, events and to-dos the new name in Outlook too, and the old name leaves Outlook's list once they have it; mail older than what MailCraft keeps offline, events outside the days the calendar keeps here or in a calendar you may only read, and contacts keep the old name there, and when only the letter case changes, your mail there keeps the old spelling. A Microsoft account's categories take Outlook's 25 colours. Keeping your tasks, rules, automatic replies and categories in step with Outlook (on a Microsoft account still on IMAP, its tasks and automatic replies) needs your permission to change your To Do lists and your mailbox settings: a new account is asked on Microsoft's sign-in page, and an account you added before is asked in one question (Keep in Step with Outlook) with one Microsoft page for all of it, in place of the separate questions there were; one that already allowed changing its rules or automatic replies isn't asked again for its categories. A category that was only on this computer stays off Outlook's list and says so, with Add to Outlook; mail, events and to-dos you give it still carry its name to Outlook, as before ([#661](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/661)).

**Public beta.** For Microsoft accounts (personal, or work or school) MailCraft is a working mail, calendar and contacts program, kept in step with Outlook on the web; keep Outlook to hand while you try it, and tell us in an issue if anything is lost or out of step. Much of what a daily mail program needs is still to come, and other kinds of account are welcome but not part of the beta. What works is in the [README](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY#what-works-today).

MailCraft for Windows, macOS, Linux, FreeBSD and the web. Every file is listed with its SHA-256 in `SHA256SUMS.txt`.

MailCraft keeps your mailbox in its settings folder and saves as you go; one window per mailbox. With a Microsoft 365 or Outlook.com account you sign in on Microsoft's page, and the sign-in is kept by the system's credential store (Windows, macOS, and the Secret Service of GNOME Keyring or KWallet on Linux). What works, and how far each part is from Outlook, is in the [README](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY#what-works-today) and in `docs/gaps.md`.

The builds are not signed with a publisher certificate yet: Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper will ask before running them.
